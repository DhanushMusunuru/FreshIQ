Build & Wiring Manual

FreshIQ

A four-compartment rotating tray that photographs each compartment in turn and classifies what it finds. One board does the vision and the web interface; a stepper indexes the tray. This is how it goes together and why it is built this way.

01 How it works

Press Start scan. The tray turns one compartment, stops, waits for the platter to settle, takes a photo, classifies it, saves the result, and repeats until every compartment has an answer.

03 Power

One 12 V supply feeds three things directly. There is no buck converter, and you do not need one.

The UNO Q's VIN pin accepts 7–24 V and has its own on-board buck that feeds the 5 V system rail. 12 V is inside that range, so it goes straight in. An external step-down module would be a part that can fail for no benefit.

Common ground

The A4988's logic ground and the UNO Q's ground must be joined. Without a shared reference, STEP and DIR are voltages measured against nothing — the driver sees noise, and the motor stutters or does nothing at all. Star the grounds at the supply.

04 Wiring the driver

This is the diagram to get right. Three of these connections are the classic ways an A4988 build fails silently.

The three that bite

1 — VDD must be 3.3 V, not 5 V

The A4988 reads logic HIGH at roughly 0.7 × VDD. Powered from 5 V that threshold is 3.5 V, and the UNO Q's outputs only reach 3.3 V — so STEP is never reliably seen as HIGH. The symptom is maddening: the motor twitches, or turns sometimes, or works until it warms up. Powered from 3.3 V the threshold is 2.31 V and there is comfortable margin.

2 — RESET and SLEEP must be tied together

Both float by default, and a floating RESET holds the chip in reset forever. Nothing moves and nothing is broken, so people replace working drivers over this. One wire between the two pins fixes it.

3 — The 100 µF capacitor is not optional

It goes across VMOT and GND, physically close to the driver. The motor's inductance and the supply leads form an LC circuit; connecting power without bulk capacitance produces a voltage spike well beyond 12 V that destroys the A4988 outright. Fit it before the first power-up, not after.

Related: never unplug the motor while the driver is powered. Same failure, same result.

Confirm the coil pairs

Wire colours vary between suppliers. Set a multimeter to continuity and probe pairs: the two wires that beep are one coil. Getting this wrong makes the motor buzz and jitter instead of turning.

05 Motor current

Set this with a multimeter before the first move. Too high cooks the driver; too low loses steps under the tray's inertia — and nothing in software can detect a lost step.

Turn the trimpot and measure between the pot's wiper and ground:

V_ref = I_max × 8 × R_sense
Sense resistorsMarkedV_ref for 1.0 A
0.05 ΩR0500.40 V
0.10 ΩR1000.80 V
0.20 ΩR2001.60 V

The two small resistors sit beside the chip and carry the marking. 1.0 A is a good starting point — the 17HS4401 is rated 1.68 A, but this tray is light and turns slowly, and running cooler costs nothing here. If the tray stalls or skips, raise it in 0.1 A steps.

Heat

Above roughly 1 A the A4988 needs its heatsink and some airflow. The enclosure has side vents for this. If the driver is too hot to touch, lower the current before doing anything else.

06 Steps & indexing

The drive is 1:1 — the coupling hub goes straight on the motor shaft, no gearing — so one tray revolution is one motor revolution.

steps_per_compartment = steps_per_revolution × microsteps ÷ compartments
                      = 200 × 1 ÷ 4
                      = 50

Config validates this on startup and refuses to run if it does not divide evenly. At 3 compartments you would get 66.67 steps and the tray would creep out of alignment a little more every revolution — a fault that takes hours to notice and is invisible in a single scan.

There is no position sensor

No home switch, no encoder, no index mark. Absolute tray position is not physically knowable, so the software tracks a logical index that starts at compartment 1 when it boots. Turn the tray by hand so compartment 1 sits under the camera before your first scan.

If the motor ever skips steps, software cannot tell — the result would be labelled with the wrong compartment. That is a limitation of this hardware, not a bug. Correct it from the dashboard or with POST /api/motor/position.

Moves always go forward, never taking the short way round. Going 3 → 2 turns three compartments rather than reversing one, which keeps any mechanical backlash consistent so every compartment lands the same way.

07 Camera & light

The model judges food largely by colour. Everything here exists to make the light boring and identical from one compartment to the next.

Why plain white, always on

The strip is wired straight to 12 V with no control line. That is deliberate: a strip that cannot be changed cannot drift. Use white only — an RGB strip set to "warm" shifts every hue the classifier depends on, and a red-tinted tomato and a red-tinted apple are a much harder problem than they need to be.

Aim for even, diffuse light with no hotspots. A strip around the inside of the enclosure rim, pointed at the tray rather than at the camera, avoids both glare and hard shadows.

The bracket uses discrete detent holes rather than a friction slot. A 3.4 mm slot through a 3 mm printed arm is a layer-delamination line waiting to split, it creeps under vibration once the clamp relaxes, and you cannot return to the same angle twice.

09 Software map

Why the scan controller exists

ai.inspect() merges N images into one verdict. That is right for four photos of one tomato and wrong for four compartments holding four different foods — it would average a fresh apple and a rotten banana into a single answer. The scan controller inverts the loop: it calls inspect() once per compartment. The AI package itself is unmodified.

11 The model

MobileNetV2 with two output heads, exported to TFLite and run through LiteRT. Verified against the actual tensors, not the docs.

Input

160 × 160

RGB float32 in [0,1]. Verified from the input tensor.

Outputs

2 and 8

Condition and food. Neither head is quantised.

Size

2.72 MB

freshiq_model.tflite.

Runtime

LiteRT

ai-edge-litert. No TensorFlow on the board.

What it actually knows

Eight foods: apple, banana, cucumber, mango, okra, orange, potato, tomato. Two conditions: fresh, spoiled.

Anything else gets forced into one of those eight — the model cannot say "I have not seen this". Feed it a pear and you will get a confident wrong answer.

Preprocessing must not be changed

BGR → RGB, resize to 160, divide by 255. The model applies its own rescaling internally. A mismatch between training and deployment preprocessing does not throw an error — it quietly destroys accuracy while everything still looks like it is working.

13 Installing

Copy the folder to the board and run one thing.

./START.sh

First run installs the system libraries and Python packages, verifies the imports actually work, adds you to the dialout group, creates the data folders, then starts and prints the address. Every run after that just starts.

./INSTALL_SERVICE.sh

Starts on boot, restarts on crash. Watch it with journalctl -u freshiq -f.

Without any hardware

./START.sh --dev

Simulated motor, sample photos instead of a camera, everything else real. The whole scan flow and dashboard work on a laptop with nothing plugged in.

Network exposure

There is no authentication. Anyone on your Wi-Fi can open the dashboard and drive the tray. Fine for a LAN prototype; do not port-forward it. For remote access use a private tunnel such as Tailscale, which exposes nothing publicly.

15 Honest limits

Read this before showing anyone a number.

This is not a food-safety device

The percentages are the model's classification confidence, not a measurement. "Fresh classification: 96% confidence" does not mean the food is 96% fresh, and it says nothing about whether anything is safe to eat. There is no chemistry here — only the appearance of the surface facing a camera.

noPosition feedback. No home switch or encoder. Lost steps are undetectable.
noObject detection. One item per compartment. Two foods together gets you multiple_objects, not two answers.
noUnknown-food rejection. Anything outside the eight classes is forced into one of them.
noInterior assessment. Surface appearance only. A perfect-looking fruit can be rotten inside.
noteDomain gap. Trained on web photos of supermarket produce, not photos from this rig. It will do worse here than its test score suggests.
noteFDM prints are not food-safe. Layer lines trap bacteria and survive washing. The tray is for whole uncut fruit; hand-wash cool, never a dishwasher.
yesBad frames are refused rather than guessed at, with a named reason.
yesThe tray fails safe. Every error path de-energises the motor.

The dashboard's Teach it your setup panel collects photos from your actual camera and lighting. That is the single highest-value thing you can do to close the domain gap.
